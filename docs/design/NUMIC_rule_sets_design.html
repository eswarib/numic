NUMIC age-based rule sets

The plan is to replace the hand-typed VI percentile and 97th-centile reference with lookups from published age-based charts, offered as two rule sets: Levene and Brouwer. This page lists what that would change in the NUMIC repository and what it affects.

Updated: rule sets are numic_flow_levene (default) and numic_flow_brouwer, and old IDs are retired. Day of life is worked out from date of birth and scan date/time, not entered. Analysis of src/numic, tests and web/clinical-demo on branch master. Sources: El-Dib et al., J Pediatr 2020, refs 43–45. Written 5 Oct 2026.

The two rule sets

The new rule sets numic_flow_levene and numic_flow_brouwer replace today's numic_flow_v1 and numic_flow_v2_pre95, which are retired. numic_flow_levene is the default. Both share the same progression rules, clinical modifier and risk bands (0–14; low 0–3, moderate 4–7, high 8–14).

The VI percentile and VI reference fields are removed. Nobody types in a day of life: NUMIC works it out from date of birth and scan date, then works out the rest:

# day of life = scan date − date of birth (birth day = day 0) # age at scan = gestational age at birth + day of life ÷ 7 born 2 Mar 2026 at GA 27+3 → 27.43 wk scan 14 Mar 2026 09:00 → day 12 age at scan → 27.43 + 12/7 = 29.14 wk # for each metric the selected rule set treats as age-based: reference line at 29.14 wk ← straight-line interpolation between 29 and 30 wk in the table measured mm vs line → points, plus "+1.8 mm above the line" for the explanation outside the chart's range → "outside chart range" (no guessing)

Core design change

Scoring currently receives only the measurements (VentricularMeasurements) and never sees the baby's age. Age-based thresholds need age at scan passed into the static scorer:

  • Date of birth stays on PatientInfo. The field date_of_birth already exists. It changes from optional to required for scoring with age-based rule sets.
  • Gestational age at birth is stored on PatientInfo as weeks + days. The existing gestational_age_weeks is documented as "gestational age at scan", which conflicts and should be replaced.
  • Scan date and time is the existing required MeasurementContext.measured_at. No new per-scan field is needed.
  • The API works out day of life (scan date − date of birth) and age at scan for each scan, and passes age to scoring. It rejects a scan dated before the birth.
  • Each metric rule in StaticRules becomes either a fixed threshold or a reference-line threshold (table name + mm above the line for 2 points).
  • The static result returns the reference values it used (age, line in mm, distance from the line, table name), so the score stays explainable.

Impact

Blocker

Brouwer's 2-point thresholds are not defined yet. ELVIS gives two cut-offs per metric, and Brouwer gives one 97.5th-centile line per age. Scoring above the line as 1 point is clear. What earns 2 points needs a clinical decision: line + N mm, a second centile, or El-Dib Figure 4's risk zones. The same applies to VI under Brouwer.

Blocker

The reference tables are a hard dependency. Levene's VI values and Brouwer's VI, AHW and TOD values must come from the original papers or El-Dib's linked spreadsheets (check their terms of use first). They are clinical content and need a clinician to check them.

Behaviour

Scores change for the same input. Thresholds now depend on age, so the same mm values can give a different band than today. The new names keep these results apart from numic_flow_v1 results, so older results stay traceable to the rules that produced them. Both names fit the database's 32-character score_version column.

Retired IDs

Requests naming numic_flow_v1 or numic_flow_v2_pre95 are rejected. get_rules already returns 422 with the list of known IDs, so callers see what to switch to. /score/versions lists only the two new rule sets. Later threshold changes are published as new revisions of the same rule set (for example numic_flow_levene@2), made through the admin editor and never by editing a published revision in place.

Breaking API

Inputs change. vi_percentile and vi_p97_reference_mm disappear, and date of birth and gestational age at birth become required for scoring. There are no outside users yet, so a clean break is cheaper than supporting both.

Coverage

Age-range limits differ. Brouwer covers 24–42 weeks, and Levene's chart starts around 26 weeks. A very premature baby scanned early may be scorable under Brouwer but not Levene. The design should say "outside chart range" rather than guess.

Simpler

Day of life is worked out, not entered. It comes from date of birth and the existing scan date/time (measured_at), so there's no new per-scan field and no risk of the two disagreeing. PatientInfo.date_of_birth already exists, and spreadsheet import and the CLI already accept it.

Privacy

Date of birth is identifying data. Storing it on patient info brings it under UK GDPR and DPIA scope for any real-patient use. On the public website demo it should only ever hold a synthetic default (for example derived from today's date), with a clear "do not enter real patient details" note.

Edge cases

Date and time zones. Date of birth is a date, while scan time is a timezone-aware datetime. Day of life should use the scan's date in the hospital's local time, or a scan just after midnight UTC could be counted as the wrong day. A scan dated before the birth is rejected.

Hosting

This affects how the website demo is hosted. If scoring runs in the browser (option A), the tables would exist in both Python and JS. Keeping them in one shared JSON file used by both removes the risk of the two copies drifting apart.

Unchanged

Progression, the clinical modifier and the risk bands stay the same. Scores still run 0–14, and the low, moderate and high bands keep their boundaries.

Combining the three layers

The final band is currently a plain sum: static (0–6) + progression (0–6) + clinical (0–2) = 0–14, with low 0–3, moderate 4–7 and high 8–14. A plain sum has two blind spots that follow directly from the code:

  • One measurement in its worst band can stay low. VI 0, AHW 0, TOD 2, no growth and mild concern gives 3, which is low.
  • A first scan can't reach high without clear clinical concern. With no prior scan, progression is 0. All three metrics in their worst band gives 6, which is moderate, even though it meets the ELVIS high threshold (VI > 97th + 4 mm and AHW > 10 mm).

El-Dib's Brouwer-based tool (Figure 4) plots each measurement on its own chart with its own moderate and high risk zones, so one abnormal measurement is visible on its own. There is also a clinical counterpoint: under the ELVIS lower threshold, a large TOD only counts together with VI above the 97th centile. Whether an isolated large TOD should be low is a clinical question.

What the comparison shows

  • D can fix the large-TOD case. All three variants move case 1 to moderate.
  • It does so by moving other babies too. D1 and D3 also move case 4 to moderate, which means more babies on tighter surveillance and more alerts.
  • D can't express "this AND that". Case 2 meets the ELVIS high threshold yet stays moderate in every variant. A sum treats all combinations with the same total alike, so making "VI high + AHW high" reach high also pushes other combinations with the same total to high.
  • A first scan is penalised in every variant. With no prior scan, up to 6 of 14 points can't be scored. This needs its own first-scan policy (see configuration below).
  • D is the right tool once there is outcome data. In established clinical risk scores, weights and cut-offs are fitted to outcomes (for example reservoir or shunt, or 2-year neurodevelopment) and then validated on separate patients. Without data, hand-picked weights only look precise.

Recommendation: use B now for the clinically known patterns, and keep D for recalibration once pilot or retrospective outcome data exist. Separately, decide how a first scan with no prior is banded. D2 and D3 would also break the current 0–14, 0–6 and 0–2 limits in api/schemas/scoring.py and demo.py, the hard-coded "Low 0–3 / Moderate 4–7 / High 8–14" labels in the demo, and the "0–14" wording in the pitch materials.

Configuration parameters

Today the only app setting is the database URL (core/config.py, NUMIC_ environment prefix). Every clinical threshold is a Python constant in rules/bundles.py. The parameters below should be configurable, in two tiers that are governed differently:

Tier 1 · clinical rule-set parameters

Change scores. Admins edit them in the rule editor (see the next section). The JSON files (for example rule_sets/numic_flow_levene.json) hold the factory values that pre-fill revision 1. A published revision is read-only; changing any value creates a draft that becomes a new revision once an approver publishes it, so past results stay traceable. They are never changed per request.

Tier 2 · deployment and display settings

Never change a score. Environment variables (NUMIC_* via pydantic-settings) for the backend and config.js for the demo. They can be changed per deployment without a new rule-set version.

Admin rule editor, clinician decisions and learning loop

Three connected features: an admin edits the rule-set values in the app, clinicians can record the band they actually decided, and over time the gap between NUMIC's band and the clinician's band (and later, outcomes) suggests better thresholds. Each feature depends on the one before it.

1 · Admin rule editor

Admins can edit every Tier 1 value in the configuration section above, pre-filled with today's values:

  • Band cut-offs: low ≤ 3, moderate ≤ 7.
  • Static layer: points per band and the thresholds for VI, AHW and TOD (fixed mm or reference-line offsets).
  • Progression layer: the mm cut-offs for VI/AHW (1 / 2) and TOD (2 / 4), and the points they earn.
  • Clinical layer: points for none, mild and clear concern (0 / 1 / 2).
  • Later: minimum-band rules and the first-scan policy, once decided.

To keep every result traceable, an edit never changes a rule set that has already produced scores. Editing creates a draft copy, and publishing turns the draft into a new revision:

  1. Publishede.g. numic_flow_levene@1, the factory values from the JSON seed file. Read-only.
  2. Edit → draftThe admin clicks Edit. A draft copy opens with every value pre-filled, and only drafts can be changed.
  3. ValidateThresholds must increase, points must stay in allowed ranges, band cut-offs must fit within the maximum score, and named reference tables must exist.
  4. Preview impactRe-score recently stored assessments with the draft and show how many would change band, and which ones.
  5. Approve and publishA second person with the approver role (e.g. the clinical lead) approves the draft with a reason. It becomes numic_flow_levene@2 and the active revision.
  6. Roll backAny earlier revision can be made active again. Old results always keep the revision that produced them.

Every step is written to an audit trail: who, when, the before/after differences, and the reason. A revision ID such as numic_flow_brouwer@12 fits the existing 32-character score_version column.

2 · Clinician decision capture (optional)

When decision capture is turned on for a deployment, the clinician can record the band they decided after seeing NUMIC's result. For example, NUMIC says moderate and the clinician decides low.

Each stored case then holds the raw measurements, age at scan, changes and time between scans, layer scores, the predicted band and rule revision, and the clinician's band with its timestamps. Storing the raw mm values matters: new mm thresholds can only be learned from raw mm, not from points.

3 · Learning loop

Phase 1 · from the first casesAgreement dashboard

Predicted band vs clinician band as a 3 × 3 table, override rate per band and per rule revision, and which metrics were abnormal in the overridden cases. This is useful long before there is enough data for a model.

Phase 2 · once enough cases existThreshold suggestions

An offline job fits an ordinal logistic regression (bands are ordered) on the raw inputs: mm relative to the reference line, change per day, and clinical concern. It turns the fitted model into suggested cut-offs and saves them as a draft revision with its evidence: case count, agreement before and after (cross-validated), calibration, and the cases that would change band.

Phase 3 · alwaysHuman approval

A suggestion goes through the same review → approve → publish path as a manual edit. It is never applied automatically. Until a configurable minimum number of cases is reached, the app shows "not enough data yet".

Things to get right

Ground truth

The clinician's band isn't the right answer, only another opinion. Training on it teaches NUMIC to copy local practice, including its variation between people. Clinicians also see NUMIC's band first, which pulls their decision toward it, so the model would partly learn from itself. Mitigations: also record outcomes (lumbar puncture, reservoir, shunt and their dates; later, neurodevelopmental follow-up) and use them as the primary target. In an evaluation study, the clinician can record their own band before seeing NUMIC's.

Data volume

A model needs enough events. A common rule of thumb is 10–20 events per model parameter. High-band cases are rare, so useful suggestions likely need many months of data, probably from several units.

Governance

This stores real patient data and reuses it. It needs a DPIA and information-governance approval. Using routine data to build a model is likely to need research approval (ethics and, in the UK, possibly HRA/CAG) or a carefully scoped service-evaluation route. Analysis should run on a pseudonymised extract.

Regulatory

Editable clinical thresholds and data-driven updates change how the device behaves. Under a software-as-a-medical-device route, sites may only be allowed to edit within validated ranges, and threshold updates need change control. Regulators' "predetermined change control plan" approach (for algorithms expected to change) is the relevant idea to raise with QMS advisers early.

Dependencies

Users, roles and migrations don't exist yet. Today there is no login, and the database tables are created without migrations. Admin, approver and clinician roles need authentication, and stored clinical data needs real migrations (Alembic).

Public demo

Keep both features out of the public website demo. No real decisions are stored there. The rule editor could appear as a sandbox whose edits stay in the visitor's browser.

Open decisions

  1. Who can approve a rule-set revisionFor example the unit's clinical lead, with the admin who drafted it unable to approve their own draft.
  2. What the learning loop learns fromClinician band only, outcomes only (lumbar puncture, reservoir, shunt, follow-up), or both with outcomes as the primary target.
  3. Approval route for storing and reusing decisionsDPIA and information governance, and whether model building runs as research (ethics, HRA/CAG) or service evaluation.
  4. How the layers combine into a bandRecommended: keep the sum and add minimum-band rules (option B), each signed off by a clinician. Keep option D for recalibration once outcome data exist.
  5. First-scan policyHow a scan with no prior is banded: progression counted as 0 (today), separate cut-offs, or a "baseline, no trend yet" flag.
  6. How Brouwer scores 2 points for VI, AHW and TODLine + N mm, a higher centile, or El-Dib Figure 4's risk zones. Needs clinical input.
  7. Local time zone for day of lifeWhich time zone defines the scan's calendar day (hospital local time, e.g. Europe/London, is the suggested default).
