The problem

Tracking a fragile brain is manual, slow, and inconsistent

In preterm babies, bleeding around the brain (IVH) can lead to fluid-filled spaces enlarging — post-haemorrhagic ventricular dilation (PHVD). Care hinges on how large those spaces are, how fast they change, and how the baby is doing.

Repetitive

Staff manually trace back through prior scans and dates to re-check every measurement against agreed cut-offs.

Inconsistent

Progression is described qualitatively — "a bit bigger" — not summarised the same way at every handover.

Time-pressured

This sustained attention competes with everything else on a busy neonatal unit.

There is no single, widely adopted summary that combines absolute size, rate of change, and clinical context for handover and audit.

Tell it as a scene: a busy neonatal unit, a baby who's had a brain bleed, a new head ultrasound just came back. To know if things are getting worse, a clinician has to dig back through previous scans and dates, re-measure against agreed cut-offs, and hold the whole trend in their head — while juggling everything else. Three failure modes, one per card: it's repetitive, it's inconsistent (the dreaded "looks a bit bigger" handover), and it's done under time pressure. The punchline at the bottom: despite this being high-stakes, there is no single, shared summary that combines size, rate of change, and clinical context. That gap is the opening for NUMIC. Resist the urge to solve it yet — just make them feel the pain. ~30 seconds.

The solution

NUMIC: one explainable risk band, from inputs you already use

NUMIC is decision support software. It turns standard head-ultrasound numbers, the change since the last scan, and a short structured note on clinical concern into a single risk band — using transparent rules we call NumicFlow.

Explainable, not a black box

The output shows how each part contributed (current size, change, clinical modifier) and which rule version was applied. The reasoning stays visible and repeatable.

Clinicians stay in charge

NUMIC does not choose treatment. Staff use the band to match local pathways — scan frequency, when to escalate, when to involve neurosurgery.
